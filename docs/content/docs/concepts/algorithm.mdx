title: The algorithm
description: How SRC contracts and compresses in one randomized sweep.

Successive Randomized Compression (SRC) [@camano2026src] computes a compressed
approximation of a product of tensor trains, such as $A\,|\psi\rangle$ for an MPO
$A$ and an MPS $|\psi\rangle$, without ever forming the exact product. The exact
product of an MPO with bond dimension $\chi_A$ and an MPS with bond dimension
$\chi_\psi$ has bond dimension $\chi_A \chi_\psi$; compressing it afterwards, as
in the usual contract-then-truncate approach, pays for that intermediate size.
SRC targets a small oversampled bond dimension $\chi_{\text{os}}$ before a final
SVD truncation to the requested $\chi_{\text{out}}$.

## Randomized range finding

The building block is the randomized range finder [@halko2011finding]: to
approximate the column space of a matrix $M$ with rank about $k$, multiply it by a
random Gaussian matrix $\Omega$ with $k$ columns and orthonormalise the result,
$Q = \operatorname{orth}(M\Omega)$. Then $M \approx Q Q^\dagger M$ with high
probability, and the cost is dominated by the product $M\Omega$.

SRC applies this idea one site at a time along the chain, with the matrix $M$
being an unfolding of the (never formed) product network.

## Sketching and truncation

By default, $\chi_{\text{os}} =
\max(\lceil 1.5\chi_{\text{out}}\rceil, \chi_{\text{out}} + 10)$.
An explicit `oversample` factor sets
$\chi_{\text{os}} = \lceil \text{oversample}\,\chi_{\text{out}}\rceil$.

1. **Left to right: sketching.** At every site the open physical legs are
   contracted with a Gaussian tensor $\Omega_j$ of sketch size
   $\chi_{\text{os}}$. Accumulating these from the left gives the sketched
   environments $C_j$, each of size $\chi_{\text{os}}$ times the product of the
   input bond dimensions. The same sketch index is shared by every site, which
   makes the sketch a Khatri-Rao product rather than a dense Gaussian matrix of
   the full Hilbert space.
2. **Right to left: orthonormalisation.** Starting from the last site, the
   environment $C_j$ is contracted with the site tensors and the projected right
   environment $S$, and a QR decomposition of the result gives the new site
   tensor as an isometry. The isometry is then folded into $S$ for the next site.
   The first site absorbs the remaining weight.

3. **Left to right: exact truncation.** The intermediate is already
   right-canonical, so no re-orthogonalisation is needed. An SVD of each centre
   tensor gives the Schmidt values at that cut. Keep at most $\chi_{\text{out}}$
   values, apply the relative [cutoff](/features/cutoff), and absorb the remaining
   weight into the next site. The output is **left-canonical**, with the norm on
   the last site. The discarded weights add in quadrature, giving the usual
   TT-SVD quasi-optimality bound for this intermediate.

`oversample=1.0` recovers the original two sweeps without the final pass, including
right-canonical output and cutoff on the sketch's QR factor.

## Cost

For an MPO with bond dimension $\chi_A$ applied to an MPS with bond dimension
$\chi_\psi$, the dominant per-site cost grows like
$\chi_{\text{os}}^2 \chi_A \chi_\psi$, compared with the $(\chi_A\chi_\psi)^3$ of
an exact contraction followed by an SVD-based truncation. The same kernel handles
MPO-MPO products and plain compression; for a [stack](/features/stacks) of trains,
the product of all their bond dimensions replaces $\chi_A\chi_\psi$.

Being randomized, SRC is not optimal: its error is somewhat above the best
rank-$\chi_{\text{out}}$ truncation, with a gap that shrinks quickly as the
spectrum decays. The [benchmarks](/benchmarks) quantify this on concrete cases.
